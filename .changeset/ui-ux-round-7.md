UI/UX round 7: much less text, the same facts (T172, from the T171 copy audit and the T170 checklist):
- Shared building blocks, one implementation each, on every page: a status chip (a word, a colour, and a symbol of its own shape, never colour alone), a number with its unit, a relative time with its exact time on hover and keyboard focus, a fold (closed by default) for every explanation and technical fact, and a copy button for ids and hashes, never the first thing in a row.
- Each page applies the audit: a few words of what it is for; labels, chips, and counts in place of sentences (*Approvals · 3*, *Needs you 2 · Working 1 · Ready 1 · Proposed 0*, *Working · 1/2 slots · 1 slot free*, *5 h · used 42 % · resets 14:20*); one wording per concept across pages (*Needs your attention*, *Starts awaiting approval*, *Approval needed*, *Automatic / Notify / Ask approval / Blocked*, *Retry failed step*, *Excluded from teams*); explanations, mechanics, and ids folded, never dropped; the plain-words failure reasons kept.
- Help: each answer folded under its question, the task lifecycle as a flow of states, and a searchable glossary whose definitions are folded (a link to a term opens and focuses it); the glossary adds *stale*, and renames *action class*, *on its way in*, *always asks*, *started from earlier work*, and *sent again* to *action type*, *merge progress*, *always requires approval*, *continued from earlier work*, and *retried after a service error*.
- Production states: a *Loading…* skeleton, *updated 12 s ago* in the header, a stale banner with when the page was read while a refresh fails, an error with **Try again** for a page that never loaded, one-line empty states with a link where there is a next step, **Stop run** behind a confirmation, **Skip to content** first in the keyboard order, a visible focus on every control, no motion under reduced motion, and prose capped at a readable line length.
